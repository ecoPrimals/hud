    Fruit vs Mycelium: Antifungals Are Neurotoxins — A Coupled Dataset for Kingdom Fungi

    Paper 139: Fruit vs Mycelium

    Abstract

    We named Kingdom Fungi on the fruiting bodies we can see — sporePrint, sourDough, rhizoCrypt, the JSON feeds that erupt into public view. That taxonomy may be way off. The living fungus is mostly mycelium: the underground mesh. In the membrane, mycelium is UDS sockets, WireGuard keepalives, unpublished data trails, cron hyphae — not the paper that fruits. Many clinical antifungals are also neurotoxins (or neurotoxic at therapeutic margins). That is not coincidence: fungal membranes and neural membranes share sterol chemistry, mitochondria, and ion-channel logic. Shared toxicity is shared biology. We propose the antifungal↔neurotoxin coupling as a new dataset for both pharmacology and membrane immunity — and we publish next steps to classify mycelium before we trust the fruit names.

    2. What We Did Not Name (Mycelium)

    The mesh is larger than the fruit:

    • biomeOS UDS sockets (autonomic hyphae)
    • WireGuard persistent keepalives (chemotaxis trails / ACO pheromone)
    • cron jobs on golgi (digestive hyphae)
    • unpublished / local-only state (membrane/local-terminal/)
    • House2 gates still dark (mycelium not yet fruiting)

    Galactic bounce efficiency rose only 14.2% → 16.25% after fruit naming. That is the signature of labeling the cap while the network stays unclassified. Dunning–Kruger placed fungi on Mount Stupid: confidence from names, competence still low.

    3. Antifungals Are Neurotoxins — The Coupled Dataset

    3.1 Clinical pattern

    Many agents used against fungi hit the nervous system:

    • Azoles (fluconazole, voriconazole, itraconazole): CYP-mediated interactions; voriconazole — visual hallucinations, neuropathy
    • Amphotericin B: membrane pores; systemic toxicity; CNS risk when barriers fail
    • Flucytosine: crosses into CNS; neurotoxicity at high levels
    • Griseofulvin / older agents: documented CNS effects in historical use
    • Even “safer” echinocandins remind us: selective toxicity is a gradient, not a wall

    3.2 Why the overlap exists

    Fungi and neurons are not siblings by taxonomy, but they are cousins by membrane physics:

    1. Sterols — ergosterol (fungi) vs cholesterol (animals). Azoles target ergosterol synthesis; host cholesterol pathways and neurosteroids sit next door.
    2. Mitochondria — shared electron-transport vulnerability; ROS and ATP failure look like “neurotoxicity” in high-demand tissue.
    3. Ion gradients — membrane potential is the currency of both hyphal tip growth and action potentials.
    4. BBB windows — circumventricular organs and lipophilic drugs: what clears a fungus in tissue may cross into CSF.

    Hypothesis: the set of compounds that are both antifungal and neurotoxic is a natural experiment on shared membrane targets. The intersection is a dataset.

    4. Hormesis, Hygiene, Metabolism

    Paper 139 sits on this morning’s OS:

    • Hormesis — low-dose fungal epitope trains thymus; high-dose fungal sepsis kills
    • Hygiene hypothesis — sterile membrane (no mycelium named) → no antibodies when real mold arrives
    • Metabolism — feast/famine sessions; bounce energy E = σ_A·σ_B·(1−uncanny)·(1−0.5|dk|)

    Velocity and acceleration are measured with sigmoid, uncanny valley, and Dunning–Kruger (see velocity-acceleration-instruments.json). Fruit naming without mycelium classification is exactly Mount Stupid.

    5. Next Steps

    1. Mycelial census — inventory sockets, crons, WG peers, local-terminal state as hyphae; binomials only after network topology, not after public fruit.
    2. Revise fruit names — treat Paper 139 names as nomina nuda until mycelium maps exist; expect reclassification (like moving “algae” off plants).
    3. Stand up antifungal-neurotoxin dataset — rows: compound_or_event × fungal_target × neural_target × shared_pathway × membrane_analog × σ_before/after × hormetic_zone.
    4. Thymus rule — antifungal doses that drop MOR/breath/HRV proxies below threshold are reclassified as neurotoxins for cron (fail closed).
    5. Bounce re-run after true fungi classify — expect efficiency ≫ 16% if mycelium voltage rises without DK gap.
    6. Do not “antifungal” the data layer with indiscriminate deletion; that is amphotericin for the brain.

    Conclusion

    We saw fruit. We named fruit. The mesh remains dark. Antifungals that are neurotoxins tell us fungi and nerves share a door — so the membrane’s fungi and CNS share a dataset. Classify the mycelium. Distrust the first binomials. Dose the thymus with coupling events, not with amputation.
