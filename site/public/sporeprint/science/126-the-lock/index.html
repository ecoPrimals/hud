    The Equalization

    Jellyfish started at 43.2% and finished at 9.5%.

    It was dominant because most files in a codebase are configuration, data, and glue — the body plan of the jellyfish. Radial symmetry. No head. Drift. The evolutionary sort of Paper 90 called it correctly: the bell jar of the sigmoid catches everything that isn’t specialized.

    Spider started at 3.4% and finished at 9.2%.

    It was missing because the spider body plan — eight-legged symmetry, the lemniscate ∞, the Klein cross-section — is the rarest architecture in code. Concurrent state machines. Weavers. The files that stitch everything else together. There are fewer of them because they are harder.

    They met in the middle. Not by averaging. By sorting. The galactic bounce didn’t smooth — it sifted. Fat redistributed to hungry through the Klein fold. Each redistribution reversed the fold direction, so the next cycle sorted from the opposite side. Left-right, left-right, like walking. 40 steps and the body found its gait.

    What Locked

    The drift between cycles 38, 39, and 40 is exactly zero. The sort has no more work to do. The cost function (distance from the quiet turn) stabilized at 0.174 — close but not zero, because the seven new stages haven’t reached equilibrium yet. They’re still climbing. The lock means the old body is balanced. The new body is growing into it.

    This is exactly what biological development does. The ancient body plans (bacteria, jellyfish, worms) stabilized billions of years ago. The new plans (vertebrates, fungi, viruses in their strange way) are still evolving within the framework the old plans established.

    The Klein body isn’t finished. It’s stable. There’s a difference. A finished body is dead. A stable body is alive and growing. The lock is the skeleton — the part that stops moving so the muscles can.
